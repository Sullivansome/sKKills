# Market and originality validation protocol

Read only when the user requests competitors, originality, evidence of demand, genre trends, commercial viability, pricing or deep research — or when a decision will materially benefit from verification and browsing is available.

## Minimum research steps

1. **Define the playable comparison.** Search for the closest competitors by primary verb and decision rule, not just matching art style, genre tag, or setting. Include direct substitutes (same player's problem), adjacent comparators (shared loop), and an instructive counterexample. Do not assume the user's inspirations are the nearest competitors.
2. **Check primary evidence.** Favor official Steam/store pages, developer gameplay footage, current screenshots, public devlogs, patch notes, pricing and dated release information. Community discussion and reviews help identify repeated pain points; treat anecdotes as anecdotes.
3. **Map overlap precisely.** For each important comparator, distinguish (a) what is identical, (b) what changes the player's actual decision, and (c) why an audience might care. "X meets Y" is not enough evidence of novelty; neither is a renamed mechanic.
4. **Separate evidence types.** Public reviews, CCU estimates, wishlists, revenue estimates, social interest, store follower counts, and critical acclaim are **not interchangeable**. A visible community does not prove addressable demand, and a high rank is affected by survival/selection bias.
5. **Identify demand and discovery path.** Name the likely audience, where they encounter the game, what existing game they will compare it to, and whether a truthful screenshot or short video explains the hook. For mobile, distinguish store search, paid ads, social creative, and platform featuring; for Steam, distinguish tags, festivals, demos, content creators and wishlists.
6. **State uncertainty and time.** Link sources with dates for mutable facts such as prices, active user metrics, ratings, product roadmaps, laws, platform policies, and emerging trends. A claimed "market gap" must account for failed/obscure examples as well as hits. If research was unavailable, do not hallucinate competitor counts or statistics.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Research **a few nearest competitors well** rather than listing dozens of vaguely related hits. An originality verdict should name one of: `mechanically distinct`, `distinct in combination`, `presentation-led differentiation`, `close derivative`, or `insufficient evidence`. State the underlying evidence and remaining ambiguity.

## Avoid these claims

- "This has never been done" based on a quick search.
- "No competitors means a blue ocean" without investigating why no one is making it.
- "Game A sold well, therefore hybrid A+B will sell well."
- "Positive reviews prove good monetization."
- "This should get N wishlists or sell N copies" without a traceable model, inputs and uncertainty.
- Citing a curated *best games* list as a representative sample of typical outcomes.

## Outcome

A brief evidence-backed competitive position, at least one surprising risk or disconfirming example if found, updated confidence, and a playable test that determines whether the difference matters to real players.
