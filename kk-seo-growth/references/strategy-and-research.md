# Strategy and research

## Identify the business and audience

Record the audience's situation, trigger, desired result, current alternative, constraints, and ability to pay. Distinguish a revenue product, acquisition resource, learning project, and portfolio. A free resource can succeed without its own checkout; explain how it serves the user's goal.

Complete: "People using [alternative] who struggle with [specific limitation] may choose [product] because [verified advantage]." If the advantage is unproven, design a test before building a comparison campaign.

For games, evaluate experience, genre fit, audience taste, demonstration, and distribution. Do not force business-software keyword patterns onto entertainment. For educational content, map the problem learners recognize before introducing jargon.

## Collect demand evidence

| Evidence | Supports | Does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Customer/task observation | Specific friction and language | Market size |
| Existing purchases/prices | A paid category exists | Demand for our offer |
| Search Console queries | Our site's observed search exposure | Complete market demand |
| Keyword volume estimate | Provider's market estimate | Certain visitors or revenue |
| Google Trends | Relative interest and seasonality | Absolute volume or willingness to pay |
| Competitor reviews | Reported strengths and unmet needs | Representative prevalence |
| Community discussion | Vocabulary and context | Purchase intent from likes |
| Actual trial/purchase | Behavior under observed conditions | Durable retention or causal attribution |

Record source, date, country, language, device if relevant, and limitations. Search volumes and difficulty scores are provider estimates. A blank is preferable to an invented number. Distinguish advertiser competition from organic difficulty.

Search competing solutions beyond identical apps: manual work, spreadsheets, built-in OS features, services, and doing nothing. Review official product pages, documentation, current pricing, availability, and actual trial behavior where authorized. Attribute untested vendor claims explicitly.

## Map intent to pages

Build a table: query/task cluster, intent, audience, current URL, observed search-result types, evidence gap, proposed action, first-value event, next action.

Classify intent as learn/diagnose, perform a task, evaluate options, purchase, or navigate/support. Keep different stages distinct.

Treat "A vs B" and "alternatives to A" as distinct only when the content and selection task differ. Merge synonyms into a strong page when they serve the same intent. Do not infer harmful cannibalization solely because two URLs receive impressions for the same query; inspect intent, performance, and which URL serves the user better.

Inspect representative current results. Record whether they favor tools, videos, store listings, local results, documentation, or comparisons. Being absent from one search tool's results does not establish absence from Google. Avoid unpermitted automated SERP scraping; use available approved search or licensed data.

## Choose opportunity and scope

Rank opportunities by audience/product fit, demand evidence, differentiated proof, reachable distribution, expected useful outcome, effort, and uncertainty. Do not select high-volume keywords solely because a tool marks them low difficulty.

Prefer the smallest useful asset capable of testing the promise. Set a time/effort budget and review date appropriate to the channel. A short direct-user test can assess comprehension and usefulness; organic search may require substantially longer observation.

For a new site with little data, start with observed problems, direct conversations, a few researched queries, and a focused demonstration. Do not turn empty analytics into failure or require a paid keyword subscription before doing useful work.

## Multi-site decisions

Keep independent products distinct when their audience, functionality, release cycle, or brand requires it. Use a personal site to introduce projects and show credible work. Organize related content where users naturally expect it.

Do not promise that a subdomain or subdirectory automatically wins SEO. Avoid migrations merely to pursue presumed authority. Before consolidation/migration, inventory URLs, traffic, backlinks, tracking, canonical rules, redirects, and ownership; prepare a redirect map and post-launch checks.

Link between the user's projects only when the destination is relevant. Do not build an indiscriminate cross-link network.

## Decision discipline

Label proposed positioning and content experiments as hypotheses. Preserve a working route to revenue while testing changes. Define a stop/revise condition for the experiment, not an unsupported verdict on the entire business.
