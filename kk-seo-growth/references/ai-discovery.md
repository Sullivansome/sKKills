# AI discovery and GEO/AEO

## Define the outcome

Keep brand mention, product recommendation, cited URL, identifiable referral, and useful action/payment separate. An educational page may succeed as a source without being recommended as a product. A product may be recommended through another site's citation. Do not collapse these into one unqualified visibility score.

## Eligibility and evidence

Apply useful content, crawlability, correct metadata, clear identity, current facts, and genuine external references. Verify provider documentation before changing crawler settings. For OpenAI, search inclusion and training preferences are distinct; do not conflate OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot, and user-triggered fetching.

Check robots directives, CDN/firewall behavior, and main content when evidence supports a problem. Do not broadly weaken security to chase citations. Eligibility does not guarantee selection.

For Google AI features, verify current Search Console settings, reports, dimensions, and metrics. Do not assume a report providing impressions also provides query-level conversion or isolated AI clicks.

## Questions and sources

Construct questions from real tasks and decisions: unbranded discovery, problem solving, comparisons, and branded factual checks. Keep branded/unbranded results separate because naming the brand changes the test.

Use Google results as one discovery source, not a reconstruction of every assistant's retrieval. Record actual sources/search actions when observable. Do not infer invisible rewritten queries from citations.

Label generated "best + category + year" queries as hypotheses. Avoid fixed claims about provider, fan-out count, training delay, source count, sentiment, ranking-position penalties, or monthly rotation without evidence of the specific product/version/context.

## Reproducible evaluation

Use assets/ai-observations.csv. Record exact prompt, product/interface, displayed model if known, date/time/timezone, language/market, session state, observable search mode, repetition, full answer location, mention, recommendation, cited URLs, and limitations.

Use a small stable question set and repeat across fresh sessions/dates. Log changes to the set. Logged-out sessions reduce some personalization but do not eliminate localization, experiments, model variation, or randomness.

Keep API and consumer-UI samples separate. Show denominators and descriptive observations. Do not call API monitoring a measurement of identical consumer behavior or promise monthly source rotation.

For before/after comparisons, preserve baseline prompts/conditions, use comparison questions/pages where practical, and record concurrent changes. A single changed answer does not establish an effect from one backlink or rewrite.

## Vendor claims

Inspect prompt sampling, market/language, model/interface, timing, repetition, source extraction, denominators, treatment/control, and downstream traffic. Distinguish citations from all consulted URLs. Large samples cannot repair biased selection or establish causation.

Treat case studies as hypotheses. State when raw data or reproducible methods are unavailable. Do not transfer French agency-ranking patterns to English consumer games without testing.

## Credible external evidence

Seek real reviews, demonstrations, research references, open-source use, and relevant editorial coverage. Supply verifiable facts and let reviewers reach their own conclusions. Choose review platforms based on the product and users; do not assume one directory fits every SaaS.

Avoid fabricated reviews, reciprocal first-place rankings, artificial mentions, and paid ranking links. Disclose sponsorship and qualify sponsored links appropriately. A third-party placement is not inherently independent.

Start with a simple observation log. Add vendors or monitoring only for a concrete decision at an acceptable cost. A pasted MCP URL is not authorization to connect it or provide credentials.
