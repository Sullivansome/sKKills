# Page playbooks

## Shared page brief

Use assets/page-brief.md. Define one task, intended audience, evidence that justifies the page, a reason to visit beyond a generic AI answer, and a useful next action. Check whether an existing URL already serves this intent.

Write an accurate answer or proposition early. Match detail to the decision; do not force a two-line answer, word count, or formulaic heading sequence. Preserve voice, visual system, and working interactions.

Support material claims with current sources or reproducible tests. Distinguish vendor-reported and observed facts. Record test environment, version, date, sample limits, and uncertainty. Avoid unsupported professional suitability, privacy, security, accuracy, or medical claims.

## Product landing page

Explain what the product does, who benefits, where it works, and what the visitor can try next. Demonstrate the core result above excessive feature detail. Keep availability, pricing, limits, and destinations consistent with the released product.

For games, show actual gameplay and controls rather than only concept art. Explain the experience and let visitors assess taste. Do not invent multiplayer, offline use, session duration, platform support, or other capabilities.

For utilities, show input, action, result, and limitations. Offer a meaningful sample or demo when feasible. Remove registration friction only when compatible with the product and authorized scope.

## Direct comparison: A vs B

Use when users genuinely choose between these options. Verify both solve the relevant task. Disclose that the publisher makes one product.

Include a conditional verdict; dated feature/pricing table; platform/workflow differences; comparable tests; situations where each wins; weaknesses of our product; migration effort; official sources; and a clear trial/purchase route.

Compare like-for-like plans, currencies, billing periods, and features. Explain asymmetric plan comparisons. Avoid a points system engineered for our product to win. Do not claim a product was tested when it was not.

## Alternatives to a product

Begin with reasons someone might switch: platform, recurring cost, workflow fit, limits, accessibility, or verified data-handling constraints. Include credible options for different requirements. Explain switching costs and what users lose as well as gain.

Create a distinct alternatives page only when its multi-option decision differs from the direct comparison. Include our product where its actual strengths fit. Do not place it first by default or demote it to appease a supposed AI rule.

## Use-case or audience page

Prefer a specific task/constraint to a demographic label. Investigate "dictate technical vocabulary into an editor" before a generic "for developers" page.

Show a realistic workflow, relevant inputs, expected output, verified capabilities, limitations, and proof. Include substantial audience-specific evidence. Do not generate near-identical pages with swapped occupation names.

For professional audiences, verify task-specific requirements before suitability claims. Do not infer compliance from local processing or claim specialist accuracy without relevant testing.

## Educational and troubleshooting page

Start with a recognizable problem. Provide a minimal reproducible example, explanation, actionable workflow, expected result, and a check that confirms success. Explain related terms when they help.

For AI coding prompts, include required evidence, constraints, desired outcome, and verification. Avoid promises of guaranteed fixes. Show failure cases and recovery.

Use real experience with explicit limits. Add a runnable exercise, tested file, interactive demonstration, or concrete artifact when it improves completion. Preserve a glossary as supporting reference where useful.

## Video and demonstration

Choose a task easier to understand on screen. Show actual use and important constraints. Match title and thumbnail to the demonstration. Prepare captions/transcript, useful chapters, and an accurate product link where appropriate.

Pair video with a useful resource. A watch page and a product page serve different intents; follow current video indexing requirements rather than assuming every embed gets a video result. Evaluate visitors and downstream actions, not views alone.

## Refresh and consolidation

Inspect the page's role, accuracy, traffic from available channels, conversion, backlinks, and user needs. Fix inaccurate information even if it ranks. Preserve useful material during consolidation and prepare redirects where appropriate.

Update dates after substantive changes. Keep valuable URLs stable where feasible. Do not refresh the title's year without checking the underlying content.

## Acceptance

Confirm distinct task coverage, supported claims, actual product fit, working links/CTAs, ownership disclosure where needed, and preservation. Treat unknown facts as omissions or qualified statements, never invented selling points.
